Anthropic shares close at $91.88, holding above $83 IPO price after a volatile first week

Anthropic official brand assets (anthropic.com)

An AI stock rally helped the Claude maker's newly listed shares finish the week comfortably above their offering price

by Vivian Nguyen

Fri 27 Nov 2026 · 17:52 EST · 3 min read

In Stocks

Share

   
 Follow on Google

Anthropic’s stock closed at $91.88 on November 27, 2026, ending its first week of trading above the $83 IPO price.

The $83 figure is what IPO buyers actually paid. For a company that just completed the largest IPO in history, trading above that price means early investors are in the money.

Shares rose as investors bought artificial intelligence stocks again after Thanksgiving, part of a wider market recovery.

From pop to wobble to rebound

Anthropic priced its offering on November 18, 2026, at $83 per share. That was above the expected range of $70 to $78, which meant demand was strong before any shares traded publicly.

The company sold 980 million Class A shares in the public offering, raising $81.3 billion. A greenshoe option, which lets underwriters sell extra shares when demand is strong, helped push that figure higher.

Trading began on the Nasdaq the following day under the ticker ANT. The stock opened at $111.50, roughly 34% above the offering price.

The stock gave back part of that gain during the session. Shares finished day one at $97.17, still up 17.1% from the IPO price, which valued the company at roughly $1.27 trillion.

On a fully diluted basis, which counts all shares that could eventually exist, the valuation peaked somewhere between $1.76 trillion and $1.8 trillion.

By the Friday after Thanksgiving, the rebound in AI stocks had carried shares back up to $91.88.

The stock now sits below its $111.50 opening print and its $97.17 first close, but above the $83 that IPO allocations cost.

The biggest IPO ever, with some famous friends

Counting a concurrent private placement, the deal generated approximately $119.5 billion in gross proceeds. That made it the largest IPO to date, surpassing the previous record set by SpaceX.

The private placement itself was worth $26 billion. It consisted of non-voting Class N shares sold to major tech companies including Google, Amazon, and Nvidia.

Seven co-founders, including CEO Dario Amodei, sold shares during the offering, turning part of their stakes into cash after years of building the company privately.

What this means for Anthropic and AI investors

The first point is the $83 line. When a heavily watched debut falls below its offering price, coverage can shift quickly from “must-own” to “overhyped.” Anthropic stayed above that price this week, at least for now.

Second, the first-day pop did not settle the price. The gap between the $111.50 open and the $91.88 Friday close shows how much of the opening price came from early excitement. Investors who bought at the open are underwater. Investors who got IPO allocations are comfortably ahead.

Third, Anthropic’s stock now appears to move with AI stocks as a group. The late-week bounce came alongside a sector-wide recovery, not a company-specific announcement.

At roughly $1.27 trillion based on the first-day close, and a fully diluted figure that peaked as high as $1.8 trillion, shareholders are paying for a lot of future growth.

Google, Amazon, and Nvidia hold non-voting stakes through the $26 billion private placement, so some of Anthropic’s business partners are now also its shareholders.

For now, the stock sits at $91.88. That is well short of the opening-bell price, but it is still above the offering price at the end of week one for the biggest debut Wall Street has ever seen.
